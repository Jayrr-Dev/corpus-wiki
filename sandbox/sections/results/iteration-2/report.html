Tablet (magazine)

Introduction: Tablet is an American magazine focused on Jewish news and culture, founded in 2009 by editor-in-chief Alana Newhouse and supported by the Nextbook foundation (C1)., It was founded as a web magazine in June 2009 in New York as a rebranded, news-focused version of the Jewish literary journal Nextbook, with Nextbook foundation support (C4)., Tablet styled itself as the first multimedia Jewish journalistic enterprise, reporting on politics and religion and hosting podcasts (C6).

  1. Founding and early recognition
    Alana Newhouse was inspired to found Tablet while researching Roman Vishniac's historic photos of the Jewish community, wanting a magazine diverse in thought for the contemporary Jewish community (C3)., In the three years after its founding, New York Magazine described Tablet as a must-read for young politically and culturally engaged Jews (C5)., Tablet received National Magazine Awards in 2010 and 2011, one for Vox Tablet and one for its blog, and won Rockower Awards in 2013 and 2022 (C7)., Tablet has won National Magazine Awards, including one for General Excellence, and has been described as conservative or center-right by Jewish organizations (C2).
  2. Editorial identity and political direction
    In September 2014, The New York Observer wrote that Tablet was known for exceptional Jewish arts and culture coverage but, after the 2014 Gaza War, frequently published right-wing defenses of Israeli policy alongside fewer left-wing pieces sympathetic to Palestinians (C12)., Acting editor-in-chief Mark Oppenheimer said there was no editorial position at all when asked about an editorial line at Tablet (C13)., After criticism in 2017 that Tablet was too liberal or too conservative, Alana Newhouse said the magazine was designed to be heterodox without any party line (C17)., On September 29, 2022, the Association for Jewish Studies paused its advertising relationship with Tablet after member complaints about its increasingly right-wing nature, citing articles critical of transgender healthcare and COVID-19 vaccines, an interview with an antisemite, and new editors supportive of Trump's policies (C18)., Tablet editors maintained the magazine still aimed to represent many viewpoints, and rabbi Shaul Magid claimed none of his articles critical of Zionism were ever censored (C19)., In January 2025, scholar Steven Windmueller noted Tablet as a publication where conservative Jewish political thought is regularly disseminated, part of a growing movement for Jewish conservatism (C20)., Sasha Senderovich and Shaul Magid became critical of Tablet after contributing to it; Senderovich left after 2017 articles in which Leibovitz defended Trump adviser Sebastian Gorka, and Magid left in 2021 after feeling his internal criticism of conservative content was ineffective (C32).
  3. Notable reporting and controversies
    In 2011 Tablet announced Jeffrey Goldberg would move his blog from The Atlantic to Tablet; Goldberg corroborated the announcement but never moved and kept publishing in The Atlantic (C8)., In May 2016, Jeffrey Goldberg attributed a negative characterization of him in David Samuels' New York Times Magazine profile of Ben Rhodes to a longtime personal grudge stemming from Goldberg's decision not to move to Tablet (C9)., In 2012, Tablet published Anna Breslaw's Breaking Bad review criticizing Holocaust survivors as villains masquerading as victims; Goldberg called for an apology and the magazine apologized (C10)., In These Times staff writer Lindsay Beyerstein called Breslaw's article the worst thing Tablet has ever published and a disgrace on every level (C11)., In July 2012, Tablet contributor Michael C. Moynihan broke the story of Jonah Lehrer's fabrication of Bob Dylan quotes in Imagine, leading to Lehrer's resignation from The New Yorker and recalls of Imagine and How We Decide by Houghton Mifflin Harcourt (C22)., Moynihan's investigation into Lehrer later became the subject of Jon Ronson's So You've Been Publicly Shamed (C23)., In October 2017, Tablet published Mark Oppenheimer's article arguing Harvey Weinstein's sexual assaults were distinctively Jewish; it was shared favorably by David Duke and Richard Spencer, and Oppenheimer apologized (C15)., Jewish Currents described Oppenheimer's Weinstein article as both supporting an antisemitic stereotype and avoiding discussion of rampant misogyny in both Jewish and non-Jewish worlds (C16)., In August 2018, during Julia Salazar's New York State Senate campaign, Tablet published an article questioning her claims of being Jewish and an immigrant; Salazar responded in a Jewish Currents interview (C24)., After the 2018 Pittsburgh synagogue shooting, Newhouse and all six editorial staff members traveled to Pittsburgh to report on the shooting and its aftermath, focusing on stories of actual human beings affected rather than big-picture think pieces (C25)., Tablet's Pittsburgh coverage included reporting on funerals of those killed and a special edition of its podcast Unorthodox (C26)., In December 2018, Tablet published an article arguing the Women's March leaders had excluded Jewish women from leadership and used antisemitic language since 2016, especially critiquing connections to Louis Farrakhan (C27)., The Women's March organizers spoke against Farrakhan's most extreme statements, issued an apology, and made organizational changes to include Jews in leadership, but did not generally condemn Farrakhan, leading to enduring backlash (C28)., In April 2021, Tablet published an article by Jay P. Greene, Ian Kingsbury, and Albert Cheng about a survey finding more highly educated people may be more antisemitic, contrary to the consensus that education reduces antisemitism (C29)., The survey used a double-standard design showing respondents one of two examples, only one related to Judaism, such as Black Lives Matter protests or Orthodox Jewish funerals for a COVID-19 gathering question; researchers argued harsher application of principles to Jews indicated antisemitism (C30)., In 2017, Tablet hired Gretchen Rachel Hammond, who had been fired from the Windy City Times after breaking the story that three Jewish women were asked to leave the Chicago Dyke March for carrying rainbow flags with Jewish stars (C34)., In February 2015, Tablet tested a monetization method where reading was free but commenting cost $2 per day, $18 per month, or $180 per year (C14).
  4. Podcasts and audio projects
    Tablet launched the podcast Unorthodox in 2015, hosted by Stephanie Butnick, Liel Leibovitz, and Mark Oppenheimer, who was later replaced by Joshua Malina; it features 'News of the Jews,' a 'Jew of the Week,' and a 'Gentile of the Week,' has been downloaded over six million times, produces a live show across the United States, and no longer produces new episodes (C35)., Tablet Studios has published podcasts including Radioactive about antisemitic radio priest Charles Coughlin, Gatecrashers about Jews in the Ivy League, and Take One, a daily Talmud page discussion podcast; from 2014 to 2022 it partnered with the podcast Israel Story on its first six seasons (C36)., Until summer 2016, Tablet hosted Vox Tablet, a National Magazine Award-winning weekly podcast launched in 2005 under editor Blake Eskin, previously known as the Nextbook podcast, featuring interviews with cultural figures like Michael Chabon and Norman Mailer, reported stories by Daniel Estrin and Gregory Warner, produced by Julie Subrin and hosted by Sara Ivry (C37)., In December 2023, the USC Shoah Foundation announced a partnership with Tablet Studios to launch a collection of audio and video testimonies from the 2023 Hamas-led attack on Israel (C38).
  5. Contributors, lists, and book projects
    Tablet's editor-in-chief is Alana Newhouse, her husband David Samuels is literary editor, Liel Leibovitz is editor-at-large, and Lee Smith is a contributor (C31)., Tablet's contributors and contributing editors include journalists Matti Friedman, Wesley Yang, and Michael C. Moynihan; fiction writers Howard Jacobson, Dara Horn, David Bezmozgis, Ayelet Tsabari, Etgar Keret, and Ben Marcus; academics including Elisa New, Bernard-Henri Lévy, Edward Luttwak, Walter Russell Mead, Norman Doidge, Jacob Soll, Michael Lind, Natalie Zemon Davis, and Maxim D. Shrayer; novelist Marc Weitzmann; critics Marco Roth and J. Hoberman; and cartoonist Jules Feiffer (C33)., Tablet published '100 Greatest Jewish Songs' in 2010, '100 Greatest Jewish Films' in 2011 with E.T. the Extra-Terrestrial in the top spot, and '101 Great Jewish Books' in 2013 including Betty Friedan, Sholem Aleichem, Karl Marx, and Art Spiegelman (C39)., In 2018, Tablet published the '100 Most Jewish Foods,' which spawned a book of the same title, and began a line of books with Artisan including The Newish Jewish Encyclopedia and a Passover Haggadah with artwork by Shai Azoulay (C40)., In May 2025, Tablet launched a monthly print edition reportedly edited by Lorin Stein of The Paris Review, with back-cover ads The Nation said promote products of Peter Thiel, Bari Weiss, and Mike Solana's newsletter Pirate Wires (C21).

School colors

Introduction: C1, C2

1. Origins in nineteenth-century England

  • C3 The tradition of school colors appears to have started in England in the 1830s.
  • C4 The University of Cambridge chose Cambridge blue for the Boat Race against Oxford in 1836.
  • C11 Using colors to identify university sports teams dates back at least to the second Oxford–Cambridge Boat Race in 1836.
  • C5 Westminster School has used pink since a boat race against Eton School in 1837.
  • C6 Durham University adopted palatinate purple for its MA hood some time before that degree was first awarded in 1838.

2. Adoption by American colleges

  • C7 Many US colleges adopted school colors between 1890 and 1910, generally choosing distinctive combinations, though many Presbyterian colleges imitated Princeton University's black and orange.
  • C8 Some American schools have adopted red, white, or blue from the national flag as school colors.
  • C9 Among US colleges ranked in the 2012 Forbes Top 50 or the 2012–13 NCAA basketball or cross-country rankings, the most popular colors were white, blue, red, black, and gold.
  • C10 The same five colors—white, blue, red, black, and gold—were the most popular in each of the three rankings individually.

3. Athletics and institutional branding

  • C12 Cambridge Blue is only one of twelve colors in the university's supporting palette, not one of its six core colors.
  • C13 The University of Nottingham uses green and gold for its sports, but blue as its overall university brand color.
  • C14 Roger Williams University changed its athletics colors in 2018 to match the university colors, aiming to foster a strong, unified visual identity for RWU Athletics.
  • C15 Most competitive teams keep two sets of uniforms in different colors so the two teams in a match can be distinguished.
  • C16 In American football, a darker color, often the school's main color, is emphasized on home uniforms, with the away team wearing a lighter color or white.
  • C17 In basketball, a lighter color is used for the home team and a darker color for the away team.
  • C18 In some sports such as association football, the home team chooses its color and the away team plays in a contrasting strip.
  • C19 Support groups for athletic teams, including cheerleaders and marching bands, wear uniforms in their school's colors.

4. Team nicknames drawn from colors

  • C21 University colors can become nicknames for sports programs, such as the Palatinate (Durham) and the African Violet (Loughborough) in the UK, and the Harvard Crimson and Cornell Big Red in the US.

5. 'Colors' as achievement awards

  • C20 At many private schools and some traditional state schools, 'colors' are awards presented for achievement in a sport.

6. School colors in academic dress

  • C22 School colors are used in the academic dress of many institutions.
  • C23 The first school color adopted by a university for academic dress was palatinate purple at Durham University, England, some time between 1835 and 1838.
  • C24 US schools that award academic hoods and follow American Council on Education guidelines use hoods lined with school colors and trimmed with velvet indicating the degree's discipline.
  • C25 Some US doctoral robes are in the granting university's colors, departing from the Academic Costume Code color of black.
  • C26 Many British, Irish and Commonwealth universities and some American universities have an academic scarf in the university's colors, usually long, woollen, and patterned only with lengthwise stripes of varying widths.
  • C27 At collegiate universities such as Oxford, Cambridge, Durham, and Lancaster, each college has its own colors and scarf.
  • C28 Non-collegiate universities such as Glasgow and Newcastle have scarf colors for each faculty.
